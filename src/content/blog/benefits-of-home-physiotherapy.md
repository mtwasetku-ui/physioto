# THE BENEFITS OF HOME PHYSIOTHERAPY

## When Home Physiotherapy May Be Useful

By Micheal Ghattas, DPT | AHPRA Registered Physiotherapist | 15+ Years Experience

Physio to Home | Last reviewed: 16 November 2026

Home physiotherapy is one way to receive assessment, treatment and rehabilitation in the environment where you live. It is not automatically better than clinic-based care for everyone. Its suitability depends on your condition, goals, home environment, access to equipment and the type of treatment you need.

For people who have difficulty travelling, need help practising everyday activities at home, or benefit from an assessment of their home environment, a home visit can be a practical and clinically useful option. Some people will benefit more from clinic-based treatment or specialist facilities. The right setting is the one that safely meets the person's clinical needs.

This article explains why. Not as a marketing exercise, but as an honest account of what the research shows and what I observe in practice every week.

---

## Benefit 1: Treatment Happens Where Recovery Actually Occurs

A home visit allows the physiotherapist to observe tasks in the setting where they occur, such as getting up from your chair, using stairs or moving around your bathroom. This can help identify practical barriers and make recommendations relevant to your daily routine.

A clinic appointment can still be appropriate, particularly when specialised equipment or facilities are needed. The right setting depends on your clinical needs, goals and preferences; a home visit is not automatically better for everyone.

---

## Benefit 2: You Attend More Consistently — and Consistency Is Everything

Regular participation and an appropriate home exercise programme can contribute to rehabilitation progress. Home visits may reduce transport-related barriers for some people, although attendance and outcomes depend on many individual and service factors.

Home physiotherapy removes the most common barriers to consistent attendance:

**Transport.** For older adults, post-surgical patients, and people living with fatigue or pain, getting to a clinic appointment requires a car, a carer willing and available to drive, money for petrol, and enough physical resilience to tolerate the journey — often 30–60 minutes each way in rural and regional Tasmania. On a difficult day, those barriers mean a missed appointment. Missed appointments mean delayed recovery.

**Pain and fatigue.** The patients who most need physiotherapy — people recovering from surgery, those managing chronic pain, older adults with significant mobility limitations — are also the patients for whom travel is most difficult. The days when a patient most needs their session are often the days when getting to a clinic is hardest. At home, the session still happens.

**Weather.** In Tasmania, winter road conditions regularly deter travel, particularly for older adults and those in rural areas. A home session is unaffected.

A home visit can remove the need for a separate journey to a clinic. Whether this improves attendance or outcomes for an individual depends on their circumstances, treatment plan and available services.

---

## Benefit 3: Your Family and Carers Can Be Part of Your Recovery

Physiotherapy doesn't happen only during the session — it happens in the hours and days between sessions, when a carer is helping you transfer from bed, when your partner is encouraging you to do your exercises, when your daughter is deciding how much to assist with the stairs.

In a clinic, family members and carers typically wait in the waiting room. They don't see the exercises. They don't understand what they should help with and what they should deliberately let you struggle through on your own. They can't ask the physiotherapist why you need to do a particular movement a particular way.

When physiotherapy happens at home, carers are present. They see the session. They learn the programme alongside you. They understand the rationale. They can ask questions in real time and receive real answers from the treating clinician.

This matters enormously in post-surgical recovery, where carer behaviour between sessions — whether they're helping too much or not enough — directly affects how quickly function returns. It matters in neurological conditions like Parkinson's disease and stroke, where carer confidence and knowledge significantly affects how much meaningful practice the patient accumulates each day. It matters in aged care, where families are often anxious about safety and need to understand what is and isn't appropriate assistance.

---

## Benefit 4: The Physiotherapist Sees What a Clinic Never Could

A home visit gives me clinical information that no amount of clinic assessment can replicate.

I can see whether your bathroom is actually safe, or whether the grab rail is in the wrong position to be useful. I can assess whether your walking frame is sized correctly for your home's door widths. I can see the threshold between your back door and the step that you've mentioned catching your foot on. I can observe you getting in and out of your actual car, not a simulated transfer exercise.

A home visit lets the physiotherapist see the actual layout, access, flooring and potential hazards. A Cochrane review found that home fall-hazard reduction programmes probably reduce the overall rate of falls by around 26%, with the strongest evidence for people at higher risk of falling. These programmes assess hazards and support practical changes in the home. A home visit can make that assessment easier, although the need for a home visit depends on the person's circumstances and the intervention being considered. [Read the Cochrane review](https://www.cochrane.org/evidence/CD013258_reducing-fall-hazards-within-environment).

## Benefit 5: No Waiting Rooms, No Clinic Exposure Risk

For some people, receiving treatment at home may reduce contact with other patients in shared waiting areas. Infection risk varies by setting and circumstances; home visits still require appropriate infection-prevention practices by the clinician and household.

A home visit avoids a shared clinic waiting room, but it does not eliminate infection risk. Clinicians should follow appropriate hand hygiene, cleaning and infection-prevention procedures during home visits.

For patients managing cancer treatment, organ transplantation, or early post-surgical recovery, this isn't a minor convenience — it's a genuine clinical consideration.

---

## Benefit 6: Choosing the Right Setting

Home-based and clinic-based physiotherapy can both be appropriate. The choice depends on the person's condition, goals, safety, required equipment and whether the home environment is relevant to the assessment. A home visit does not provide access to every facility or item of equipment available in a clinic; if those are needed, the physiotherapist may recommend another setting or referral.

Portable equipment can support many home-based assessments and exercise programmes. Some treatments require facilities or equipment that are not available in a home, such as hydrotherapy pools or specialised rehabilitation equipment. Your physiotherapist can discuss whether a home visit is suitable or whether another setting is needed.

## Who Benefits Most from Home Physiotherapy

Home visits may be particularly useful for people who:

**Older adults (65+)** — particularly those with mobility limitations, multiple comorbidities, or significant falls risk. Environmental assessment, family involvement and reduced travel may make home delivery particularly useful for some older adults, depending on their needs and preferences.

**Post-surgical patients** — recovering from hip replacement, knee replacement, spinal surgery, or other major procedures. Consistency of attendance and functional relevance of treatment are both enhanced by home delivery.

**People living with neurological conditions** — stroke, Parkinson's disease, multiple sclerosis, acquired brain injury. The importance of consistent practice in a familiar environment, combined with carer involvement, makes home physiotherapy particularly well-suited to this population.

**People in rural and regional areas** — when travel or transport barriers make clinic attendance difficult. A home visit may make it easier to participate in planned rehabilitation, but outcomes depend on the person's needs, treatment plan and access to any required equipment or facilities.

**People managing chronic pain or fatigue** — for whom travel is a significant energy expenditure on high-symptom days when the session is most needed.

## Funding for Home Physiotherapy

Funding and eligibility depend on the specific scheme, plan and provider requirements. A home visit is not automatically covered under every funding pathway, so confirm the relevant approval, referral and service arrangements before booking.

**Medicare** — eligible people with a chronic condition and complex care needs may access up to five individual allied health services per calendar year under a GP Chronic Condition Management Plan. The five services are shared across eligible allied health professions. GP Management Plans and Team Care Arrangements in place before 1 July 2025 may continue until 30 June 2027. A valid referral and Medicare requirements apply.

**My Aged Care** — Support at Home replaced the Home Care Packages Program on 1 November 2025. Physiotherapy may be funded as a clinical support where it is included in the person's approved services; approved clinical supports such as physiotherapy have no participant contribution. CHSP continues separately for eligible older Australians and has been extended to 30 June 2029.

**NDIS** — physiotherapy is funded under Improved Daily Living supports for eligible NDIS participants. Both plan-managed and self-managed participants can access home physiotherapy.

**DVA** — eligible veterans can contact us to discuss physiotherapy, referral and approval requirements for their circumstances.

**Private health insurance** — rebates depend on your fund and policy. Check whether your cover includes home visits and confirm the current claiming arrangements with your insurer.

**Private pay** — for patients without a specific funding pathway, self-pay home physiotherapy is available.

## Frequently Asked Questions

### Is home physiotherapy available throughout Tasmania?

Physio to Home provides mobile home-visit physiotherapy across Tasmania, including northern, north-western, southern and regional communities. Appointment availability depends on the address, clinician capacity and travel arrangements. Contact us with your suburb or postcode so we can confirm whether a visit can be arranged.

### Do I need a GP referral for home physiotherapy?

No. You do not need a GP referral to book home physiotherapy with Physio to Home. You can contact us directly. A GP referral is required for eligible Medicare-subsidised allied health services under a GP Chronic Condition Management Plan. Private physiotherapy appointments generally do not require a GP referral.

### How long are home physiotherapy sessions?

Initial assessments are typically 45–60 minutes. Follow-up sessions are generally 45 minutes. Session length is confirmed at the time of booking.

### Can home physiotherapy help with the same conditions as clinic physiotherapy?

Many musculoskeletal, neurological and aged-care rehabilitation needs can be addressed during a home visit, but not every condition or treatment is suitable for home delivery. Some interventions require facilities or equipment that are not available in a home, such as hydrotherapy pools or specialised rehabilitation equipment. Your physiotherapist can assess your needs and discuss the most appropriate setting.
