# NDIS FUNCTIONAL CAPACITY ASSESSMENT IN TASMANIA: WHAT IT INVOLVES

## What Is a Functional Capacity Assessment, and Why Does the NDIS Ask for One?

By Micheal Ghattas, DPT | AHPRA Registered Physiotherapist | 15+ Years Experience

Physio to Home, Launceston and all of Tasmania | Last updated: November 2026

A Functional Capacity Assessment (FCA) is a formal written report from an allied health professional, most often a physiotherapist or occupational therapist. It documents how a person's disability affects their function and independence in daily life. For NDIS participants, an FCA is one of the most useful pieces of evidence the National Disability Insurance Agency (NDIA) can consider when it assesses an application, reviews a plan, or decides on equipment and home modifications.

If you've been told your plan review needs "more evidence," or a support coordinator has suggested a Functional Capacity Assessment, this guide explains what the assessment involves, who can complete one, how it is funded, and how to prepare.

Physio to Home is based in Launceston and carries out assessments in participants' homes across Tasmania, from Hobart and the south to the Tamar Valley, Devonport, Burnie and the north-west.

### Who this guide is for

NDIS participants, families and support coordinators anywhere in Tasmania who need an FCA for a first application, a scheduled or unscheduled plan review, or a request for more therapy, equipment or home modification funding.

## Why the NDIA Requests Functional Capacity Assessments

The NDIA funds supports that are "reasonable and necessary," so it looks for evidence of need. A diagnosis alone, such as multiple sclerosis, cerebral palsy or a spinal cord injury, says very little about how a condition affects someone day to day. Two people with the same diagnosis can have very different levels of function.

An FCA closes that gap. It turns a diagnosis into practical, measurable information: how far someone can walk, whether they can use stairs safely, how much help they need to transfer from bed to chair, and which supports would improve their independence. That is the detail an NDIA planner needs to make an informed funding decision.

Functional Capacity Assessments are commonly requested for:

- **First-time NDIS applications**, to establish a baseline understanding of functional need
- **Scheduled plan reviews**, to demonstrate progress, decline or ongoing need
- **Unscheduled plan reviews**, where circumstances have changed significantly since the last plan
- **Requests for increased therapy funding**, where current support hours are not enough
- **Equipment and assistive technology requests**, such as wheelchairs, walkers or pressure-relieving equipment
- **Home modification applications**, including ramps, bathroom modifications and rail installations

## Physiotherapy or Occupational Therapy: Which Assessment Do You Need?

Both physiotherapists and occupational therapists can complete an FCA. The right choice depends on what the NDIA needs to understand about your situation.

- **Physiotherapy FCA** — focuses on movement, strength, balance, mobility, endurance and falls risk. It suits requests for therapy hours, mobility supports and falls prevention, and plan reviews where physical function has changed.
- **Occupational therapy FCA** — focuses on daily living tasks, home accessibility, routines and participation. It suits personal care, household tasks, equipment, assistive technology and home modification requests.

Many participants benefit from both, particularly when a request involves equipment or home modifications. Physio to Home provides physiotherapy and occupational therapy functional capacity assessments, so we can point you to the right one, or arrange both, after a short phone call.

## What Happens During a Functional Capacity Assessment at Home

At Physio to Home, assessments are carried out in the participant's own home wherever possible. That is usually the environment the NDIA most needs to understand. Seeing someone in their actual living space captures barriers a clinic cannot: the height of the front step, the layout of the bathroom, the distance from bedroom to kitchen.

A typical assessment includes:

**1. Interview and history** — we talk through the participant's diagnosis, goals, current supports and how their function has changed over time.

**2. Standardised outcome measures** — objective, repeatable tests of strength, balance, mobility and endurance. These give the NDIA measurable data rather than a subjective description.

**3. Observation of functional tasks** — we watch the participant complete real tasks relevant to their goals, such as walking through the home, transferring in and out of a chair or bed, using stairs, or managing personal care where appropriate.

**4. Environmental assessment** — we identify physical barriers in the home and whether equipment or modifications would meaningfully improve safety or independence.

**5. Written report** — a structured document summarising the findings and functional limitations, with specific, evidence-based recommendations tied to the participant's NDIS goals.

The visit itself usually takes 60 to 90 minutes.

## What Should Be Included in an NDIS Functional Capacity Assessment Report

Not all reports carry the same weight with the NDIA. A strong FCA report typically includes:

- Clear links between the participant's diagnosis and their functional limitations
- Objective measures (standardised tests, not just descriptive impressions)
- Specific recommendations tied to NDIS goals, such as "12 months of weekly physiotherapy to address gait deficits and reduce falls risk," rather than a vague statement that "physiotherapy would help"
- Clinical reasoning that connects each recommendation to the evidence gathered during the assessment
- Where relevant, a comparison with a previous FCA to show change over time

A vague or poorly structured report can lead to a request being declined or under-funded. That is not because the need isn't real, but because the evidence wasn't presented clearly enough for the NDIA to act on.

## How to Prepare for a Functional Capacity Assessment

A few simple steps make the assessment more accurate and more useful:

- **Have your current NDIS plan on hand**, along with any previous reports or assessments
- **Think through your goals in advance** — what do you want therapy, equipment or modifications to help you achieve?
- **Note any changes since your last plan** — new symptoms, a recent fall, a hospital admission or a change in your living situation
- **Include family or carers** where appropriate — they often notice changes the participant may not think to mention
- **Have your support coordinator's details ready**, so the report can be shared as soon as it is complete

## Frequently Asked Questions

### How much does a Functional Capacity Assessment cost, and is it NDIS-funded?

The fee depends on the type of assessment, the time needed for the visit and report, and any travel. We give you a clear quote before the assessment goes ahead, so you know the cost upfront. Fees for NDIS-funded supports must sit within the NDIS Pricing Arrangements and Price Limits.

Whether the NDIS pays depends on where you are in the process. If you already have a plan, an assessment may be able to be funded from Capacity Building — Improved Daily Living, provided your plan includes therapy funding. Your support coordinator or plan manager can confirm this. If you are applying for the first time, you don't have a plan yet, so the assessment is usually arranged and paid for privately. Call us for a quote before you book.

### How long does an FCA take, and how quickly will I get the report?

The visit itself usually takes 60 to 90 minutes. We aim to provide the completed written report within one to two weeks. This can take longer if the situation needs more detailed follow-up or additional standardised testing.

### Can a Functional Capacity Assessment be used to increase my plan funding?

Yes. FCAs are commonly used to support requests for increased funding at plan review, particularly where your needs have changed or your current support hours are not enough. The NDIA makes the final funding decision, so no assessment can guarantee an outcome, but a clear, well-evidenced report gives your request the best chance.

### Does the report go directly to the NDIA, or to me first?

The report goes to the participant (or their nominee) first. From there it can be shared with your support coordinator or Local Area Coordinator, or submitted as part of your plan review documentation.

### Do I need a referral?

No referral is needed. Participants, families and support coordinators can contact Physio to Home directly to arrange an assessment.

### Should I choose a physiotherapist or an occupational therapist?

It depends on what you need the report to show. Physiotherapy suits questions about movement, strength, balance and therapy hours. Occupational therapy suits daily living tasks, equipment and home modifications. If you're not sure, call us and we'll help you decide.

### Do you assess people outside Launceston?

Yes. We visit participants across Tasmania, including Hobart, the Tamar Valley, Devonport, Burnie and surrounding areas. Any travel costs are included in your quote, so there are no surprises. For more remote areas, contact us and we'll confirm availability.
